Version: 1.1

PURPOSE
This file is the authoritative project roadmap and progress marker for this chat and project. It records the fixed highest-level project structure, the currently approved Phase 1 steps, and the rule governing when later phase steps may be planned.

GOVERNING PROJECT STRUCTURE
The project consists of exactly these six highest-level phases, in this order:

Phase 0 — Master Parameters
Phase 1 — Solar Collection System
Phase 2 — RCR Electrical Conditioning
Phase 3 — Microwave Generation, Transmission and Orbital Distribution
Phase 4 — Terrestrial Reception and Grid Integration
Phase 5 — Integrated System Simulation

These six phases represent the highest overall view of the project. Do not create, rename, split, merge, or introduce other project-level phases. Do not use the word “phase” for any lower-level work, subsystem, task group, milestone, or subdivision. Lower-level work is organized as numbered steps within its governing project phase.

PHASE 1 — SOLAR COLLECTION SYSTEM
The following ten steps are the established Phase 1 plan. Step 1 and Step 2 remain unchanged. Step 3 is closed by the user's explicit authorization, with its unfinished technical work transferred into Step 4. Steps 4–10 have been revised as expressly authorized by the user to accommodate that transfer and maintain a coherent scope and dependency order.

Step 1 — Establish the Governing Inputs and Assumptions Baseline
- Register physical constants, material properties, design assumptions, and operating inputs.
- Establish input-readiness checks and the independent operating-state structure for Pole 1 and Pole 2.

Step 2 — Establish Natural Force and Torque Equilibrium
- Calculate solar radiation pressure and gravitational forces.
- Complete mass accounting and natural vector-force and torque equilibrium.
- Evaluate PV coverage and collector-mass effects.
- Establish exact-equilibrium calculations and validation.

Step 3 — ICARUS Redesign Sizing and Dynamic Verification
- Step status: CLOSED BY EXPLICIT USER AUTHORIZATION.
- This closure is an administrative plan decision; it does not assert that all technical calculations, source execution, dynamic tests, or hardware qualification checks passed.
- All unfinished technical work formerly assigned to this step is explicitly carried into Step 4 and must be completed there before proceeding to Step 5.
- No remaining Step 3 technical task may be silently dropped or treated as verified solely because this step is closed.

Step 4 — Complete ICARUS Carryover, Collector Geometry, and Distributed Physical Loading
A. Complete all unfinished technical work carried forward from Step 3 before Step 4 can be accepted:
- Calculate station-level force and torque requirements independently for each pole, using the applicable collector geometry and load distributions.
- Complete the mission-specific ICARUS redesign sizing, retaining clear distinctions between modeled redesign assumptions, derived requirements, and hardware capability that has actually been qualified.
- Complete four-station force allocation, wrench balance, torque allocation, and load-transfer calculations.
- Verify membrane loading and the transfer of station forces and torques into the collector structure.
- Evaluate actuator capacity and saturation against the modeled mission requirements.
- Evaluate torque/gearing, stroke, slew rate, bandwidth and phase margin, duty cycle, power and energy, thermal limits, lifetime/radiation exposure, and attachment/load-transfer requirements. Record any item that remains an external qualification requirement rather than claiming it is qualified.
- Complete the required quaternion attitude, rigid-body response, rotation, wobble, stability, and recovery checks for the redesigned control/actuation model.
- Verify pole-state isolation and independently execute the relevant calculations and dynamic tests for Pole 1 and Pole 2.
- Correct and retest failures. Step 4 cannot be accepted while any transferred Step 3 technical work remains incomplete or unverified within the defined model scope.

B. Complete the collector geometry and distributed physical-loading work:
- Refine the three-dimensional collector representation, including curved-sheet geometry, local surface normals, patch areas, and mass distribution.
- Calculate local solar incidence from the local surface normals and Sun direction; do not substitute a flat average cell-angle assumption for the local geometry model.
- Calculate patch-level optical orientation and distributed solar radiation-pressure and gravitational force and moment contributions.
- Connect patch-level loads to the collector structural model, membrane load paths, station locations, actuator demand, and attitude/dynamics model.
- Reconcile geometry-derived loads with the carried-forward ICARUS force allocation and dynamic checks.

Step 5 — Complete the Material, Membrane, and Structural Architecture
- Develop the ultrathin aluminum collector architecture using defensible material properties and clearly identified synthetic assumptions where real-world data are unavailable or insufficient.
- Complete structural load paths, membrane behavior, stiffness, strength, stability, and load transfer using the geometry and distributed loads established in Step 4.
- Link the minimum viable aluminum thickness to the physical spacing and span of electrical wiring runs that act as load-bearing ribs; do not add aluminum thickness without a demonstrated structural need.
- Define the structural interfaces for the two-tier wiring architecture, including intra-node connections and inter-node bus runs, while leaving detailed electrical sizing and loss calculations to Step 7.
- Evaluate anti-sunward backside thin-film reinforcement only where its combined strength-to-weight and rigidity are better than the applicable aluminum-only alternative.
- Complete mass accounting for aluminum, reinforcement, wiring-support structures, interfaces, and all other modeled structural components.
- Remove superseded material concepts and obsolete references from the applicable Phase 1 model and documentation.
- Reconcile changes in mass, stiffness, load paths, force/torque equilibrium, and ICARUS requirements; record any updated loads as changes to the finalized design rather than reopening Step 3.

Step 6 — Complete Optical and Thermal Behavior
- Establish the optical properties needed by the collector model, including reflectivity, absorptivity, and emissivity for each relevant surface/material and wavelength or operating band where the model requires it.
- Propagate solar distance, Sun direction, local surface normals, incidence, and illumination across the collector geometry.
- Calculate absorbed, reflected, and otherwise accounted-for energy consistently with the optical model.
- Complete coupled radiative and conductive thermal calculations for PV cells, aluminum, substrate, reinforcement, wiring, and other relevant components.
- Determine thermal equilibrium and transient response where required by the operating model.
- Establish temperature-dependent constraints and feed resulting temperatures and material-property changes into the structural, electrical, and control models.

Step 7 — Complete Photovoltaic Collection and Electrical Production
- Complete PV coverage, cell layout, cell sizing, and node configuration using the geometry and thermal constraints established earlier.
- Complete intra-node cell connections and low-mass tabs/solder or other modeled connection methods.
- Complete inter-node bus wiring, including exact run lengths, conductor sizing/gauge, spacing, cumulative current, voltage drop, resistance, and thermal constraints.
- Evaluate series/parallel topology, current and voltage at relevant collection and distribution points, and electrical operating limits.
- Calculate I²R losses and their contribution to wiring and collector heating; reconcile them with Step 6 thermal results.
- Account for PV, wiring, connection, and support mass and propagate the resulting mass changes into equilibrium, structural loads, and ICARUS requirements.
- Calculate gross and net electrical production separately for each pole, clearly separating generated power from losses and usable/exportable power.

Step 8 — Complete Integrated Rotation, Control, Disturbance, and Operating Behavior
- Integrate the geometry, distributed loading, finalized material/structural properties, optical/thermal behavior, and PV/electrical mass and operating effects from Steps 4–7.
- Complete the integrated three-dimensional rigid-body and quaternion dynamics, natural rotation, attitude behavior, and wobble model.
- Integrate ICARUS control response, actuator dynamics, station allocation, and environmental disturbances using the finalized design properties.
- Re-run and verify the Step 4 force-allocation, actuator-limit, membrane-load, stability, and recovery results against the fully integrated configuration; this is final-design integration verification, not unfinished Step 3 carryover.
- Evaluate transient response, control stability, saturation margin, energy/power demands, thermal interactions, and long-duration operating behavior.
- Verify independent operating states, scenarios, disturbances, controller states, and results for Pole 1 and Pole 2. Do not combine their mechanical equilibrium or operating states.

Step 9 — Perform Comprehensive Coupled Optimization
- Optimize mutable design and operating parameters together, using the integrated models and constraints established in Steps 4–8.
- Include solar distance, PV coverage, cell and node counts, electrical topology, wiring gauge/length/spacing, aluminum thickness, reinforcement, material properties, structural parameters, thermal properties, and control settings where mutable.
- Trace direct and propagated effects among distance, illumination, PV area, current, conductor sizing, wiring spacing, aluminum thickness, mass, optical behavior, thermal equilibrium, structural response, and ICARUS demand.
- Maximize useful electrical power subject to each pole's own natural force and torque equilibrium, thermal limits, structural constraints, actuator/control limits, and operating requirements.
- Use coupled sensitivity and parameter-interaction analysis; tighten and relax search tolerances systematically and document bounds and assumptions.
- Re-evaluate candidate designs across the required operating scenarios and disturbances, correcting and retesting failures.
- Report each pole independently; combined totals are reporting-only and must not imply a shared mechanical equilibrium or shared operating state.

Step 10 — Complete Integrated Verification, Co-Simulation, and Phase 2 Handoff
- Load, execute, debug, correct, test, and verify the complete Mathematica-native Phase 1 source and its integrated calculation sequence.
- Establish a reproducible, verified native baseline for the completed Phase 1 model, including both independent poles and the accepted optimized design.
- Complete regression, numerical consistency, parameter propagation, pole-isolation, and end-to-end acceptance checks. Correct and retest failures before acceptance.
- Profile actual native simulation workloads only after the reproducible native baseline exists, and identify computational bottlenecks from measured execution.
- Only after the native baseline is established, design and implement justified external co-simulation workers for suitable workloads.
- Verify persistent, concurrent, bidirectional communication, synchronized simulation state/time, consistency and convergence checks, error handling, reproducibility, and external results against the native baseline.
- Complete Phase 1 acceptance and prepare the required machine-readable technical and parameter handoff to Phase 2 — RCR Electrical Conditioning.
- Only after Phase 1 is fully completed and accepted, plan Phase 2 and define its required steps as part of the Phase 1-to-Phase 2 handoff. Do not predefine Phase 2 internal steps before that point.

PLANNING AND HANDOFF CONTROL
1. The six-phase project structure is fixed and is the only project-level phase structure.
2. Do not define or pre-plan the internal steps of a later phase before its handoff-planning point.
3. The next phase’s internal steps are planned only after the preceding phase has been fully completed and accepted.
4. During that handoff, plan the next phase and add only the steps required to complete that next phase. Do not alter completed phase plans or unrelated roadmap content.
5. The same rule applies at every phase boundary throughout the project.
6. During work within a phase, the roadmap’s progress marker may be updated to show the actual current phase, current step, and verified completion status. Such progress-marker updates do not authorize changes to the established phase structure or other step definitions.
7. Phase completion must be based on completed work and verified acceptance, not merely on the existence of code, calculations, or preliminary results. An explicit user-authorized administrative closure must be labeled as such and must not be misrepresented as technical verification.
8. The user may expressly authorize a specific plan revision. The present revision is limited to closing Step 3 administratively, transferring its unfinished technical work into Step 4, and revising Steps 4–10 to provide a coherent scope and dependency sequence. This specific authorization does not permit further discretionary restructuring.
9. The roadmap is the project’s authoritative location marker. Consult it before continuing project work and maintain it under these controls.

CO-SIMULATION ORDER
For every phase involving simulation, complete and verify the native Mathematica implementation first. Establish a reproducible native baseline before profiling workloads or implementing external workers. External co-simulation must support justified workload separation, persistent concurrent bidirectional communication, synchronized state/time, consistency and convergence checks, error handling, reproducibility, and validation against native results. Mathematica remains the central mathematical and engineering authority. Do not implement co-simulation ahead of the verified native baseline.
